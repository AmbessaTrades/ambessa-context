# The venture — what is chosen and what is not

Not a company yet. No name. What follows is the position being built, stated in the order it
was decided.

## Chosen

- **One venture only.** The personal brand and the company brand are the same bet seen from two
  angles. No side projects, no looking left or right.
- **Ambi's own face and voice are the product.** Not an avatar, not a synthetic presenter, not a
  faceless channel. He records on a phone; the design system carries the polish.
- **Two buyers.** Governments in Africa and the Caribbean, and companies doing $10M–$5B.
- **High ticket only.** The floor is $5k. No consumer subscriptions, no $30-a-month tool.
- **Content is the sales channel.** One video a day, several posts a day, replies under people
  who can actually decide something. There is no ad budget.
- **Evidence over assertion.** Every claim in a video, a post or a document traces to a source
  that a stranger can re-open.
- **The target is the first million from this**, not from ads, sponsorships or another software
  experiment. YouTube currently covers the rent; the income is expected here.

## Under test — these are hypotheses, not decisions

- **The first offer.** Five candidates exist, scored, none chosen: verify a company before
  signature; verify a state before signature; a recurring document gate for firms that ship;
  commitment tracking for investment promotion agencies; one shared file both sides live in.
- **The price.** $5k–$25k to onboard one client, plus a $3k–$5k monthly retainer, is the working
  band. Nobody has been charged it.
- **Who buys first** — a company or a government.
- **The name**, and whether the company brand is separate from his name.

## The position, in one paragraph

A diasporic African who actually knows the files: who owns the company across the table, whether
the ministry can sign and pay, what paperwork clears the border. He publishes the evidence in
public, every day, with his face and his voice on it, and he sells the verified file to the two
sides of a deal that currently have no reason to trust each other. Governments buy because a
donor audit will ask; companies buy because a signature is expensive to get wrong. The content
makes the market; the file takes the money.

## What would make this wrong

- Cheaper, faster verification appears and the file stops being scarce.
- The two-sided sale proves impossible to keep confidential and one side walks.
- The daily output cannot be sustained without Ambi in the seat every day.
- The buyer turns out not to be the person who signs.

Say which of these is happening before recommending a change of direction.
