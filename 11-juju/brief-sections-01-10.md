# Master brief, sections 01 to 10, summarised

The brief is Ambi's pasted spec of 24 Nov 2026; later decisions (ADRs 0001 to 0009) outrank it.

## §01 — Purpose, north star and lexicon

Juju is a private tool for two operators. It turns a channel profile, called a Vessel, into finished faceless YouTube films. Speed outranks everything: three studio films should need under 15 minutes of human taps, plus under 90 seconds of automatic pre-render work. A live daily export milestone is set for the evening of 25 Nov 2026. The section copies four growth habits from a working channel. Move a topic from a small audience to a larger one (Rising River). After 12 uploads, start mining viewer comments for sequel demands (Graft to Bloom). When a video reaches 2.0 times the channel median, schedule sequels at 90, 70 and 50 percent similarity (Echo Decay). Never let neighbouring thumbnails look alike. The product lessons from the older channel's decline are clone fatigue, long negative-prompt lists that caused fixation, and asking image models to draw text. Core terms: Crossroads Chain (four-step causal outline); Ripple Sight (second- and third-order consequences); Crown Weight (click value split 70/20/10 across title idea, silhouette and polish); Shadow Sieve (drops words that also appear in a donor's flops); Spirit Tongue and Clean Calabash (voice rules and anti-AI-phrase filter); Resting Shelf (cooldown memory); Ancestral Breath (reasoning budgets and a 0.9 to 1.5 second pause after numbers); Value Alchemy (companion-product copy). eRPM is AdSense RPM plus 1,000 times bridge click-through, times offer conversion, times net order value.

## §02 — Audit, builder laws and bans

Twenty-two earlier builds were audited. Twelve produced working code, but only one reached strangers. The main failure was the old TypeScript codebase, which grew to about 197,000 lines of patches on patches, while its small Rust engine did the real video work. Juju therefore never imports the old server or UI. Each subsystem (Diviner, Sigil, Reliquary, Sim, Render, Offering) implements one Rust trait and talks only through versioned, hashed JSON files in a fixed chain from idea to rendered file. A new channel is a new vessel.json, never a code branch. Three behavioural traps are named: stopping short of publishing, rebuilding the whole engine over one weak layer, and trusting self-scores. Ten builder laws follow. Report measured denominators, not adjectives. Run each channel from one button. State the exact defect and cost. Keep home screens to 40 words at rest. Keep one master brief and one memory store. Use real archival photos only for named real people. Turn each defect into a code gate the same day. Batch human questions into sets of 10, 30 at most. Test UI and pipeline work in a calibrated dry-run mode with zero paid spend. Seven hard bans cover vertical Shorts, a retired topic category, paid ads, synthetic talking presenters, local language or speech models (Earshot's listening models excepted) and certain model families.

## §03 — Operators, offline sync, APK and the daily ritual

Juju ships as a Mac app, a web app and a standalone Android APK, all built from the same Rust crates through Tauri v2. Each operator profile unlocks with a local PIN that works offline. Every write commits to on-device SQLite first, then syncs in the background to a Turso replica, so a weak connection never blocks a button. The APK must run its full pipeline on the phone with a peak RAM ceiling of 580 MB. Video is encoded in five-to-seven-minute chunks, each checkpointed so a crash resumes at the last complete act, then joined in a lossless concat. Phone output must match desktop output whether or not the Mac is on. The section defines a daily ritual of five one-handed actions. Deal and Bend: swipe seven pre-forged title and thumbnail cards, or bend a competitor outlier into three variants, then preview it in Feed Sight. Seal: choose runtime band, audio mode and an optional one-sentence steer, read the exact cost (about $0.04 silent, $0.18 to $0.38 voiced), and confirm. Watch: APPROVE stays locked until 0:00 to 0:40, one 15-second middle span and the final 20 seconds have played. Swap or trim: three one-tap edits per scene card, with no timeline. Approve and Publish Kit: a one-second hold writes the export folder and opens copy-ready title, chapters and pinned-comment text.

## §04 — Dual-viewport Doop loop, skins, Earshot, Mirror Bend and Canopy

Every screen is designed as twin frames: web at 1440 by 900 and phone portrait at 390 by 844, sharing one token sheet and one database state. The Doop loop runs in two passes. Pass A copies the functional behaviour of the reference app's V1 and V2: @mention pills, looks, outlier drawer and pre-flight gate. Pass B applies the witch-theme visuals. The default skin, Crossroads Nocturne, uses near-black obsidian with spectral jade (#3BF0B8) as primary accent and nocturnal plum for secondary accents. Charred Codex and Midnight Atelier are alternative dark skins; Daylight Parchment is the light skin for glare. Typography pairs a carved display face for titles, a sans for buttons and a monospace for stat pills. The witch-cat spirit logo is the only avatar, and the spec forbids generated human or chibi characters. Earshot is the whole sound engine. For each sound it runs make (from a catalogue), build (from named recorded parts when no design exists), fit (turning an owned recording into a design), then listen and gate. The candidate gate is a fit score of at least 3.0 with no objection from the second listener; otherwise the cue goes silent. Ambi said sound must not sound like cheap synthetic effects. Mirror Bend takes a reference thumbnail's geometry, never its pixels, and produces three variants for about $0.05. Canopy is each channel's skill tree: trunk vitals plus five boughs, with blossoms for title equations, dialects, spines, ghost-onion versions and offer results.

## §05 — VidRush V1.5 extraction, blueprints and the director compiler

This section maps a reference app's screens onto Juju. Eleven rows pair each observed mechanic with its hidden engineering truth and the Juju build. Kept: the channel home (The Altar), the @mention composer (Cauldron Composer, with audio mode, runtime band and scenario continuation), ready-made looks (upgraded to Arcana loadouts on rigs C1 to C9 and dialects D1 to D6), clause-based cutting, the sourcing waterfall, and the pre-flight receipt (The Pact). Dropped: percentage sliders and the manual multi-track timeline. The timeline becomes Spell Strip scene cards and a one-tap Publish Kit. A Mermaid diagram places four surfaces over a Rust core. Diviner handles ideas and scripts, Sigil thumbnails, the clock, Reliquary and Sim footage, Offering companion products, and Render encoding. The director compiler handles voices that speak at different rates. Each bound voice is measured once from a 10-second preview, storing syllable, comma and period timings. A calibrated dry-run clock builds the word clock from those numbers at zero cost. Three audio modes share one data contract: full narration (timestamps from the voice service), sparse radio (about 80 to 85 percent kinetic subtitle cards, voice only on tagged transmission beats), and silent typographic cinema (no voice). Card hold time follows a reading formula: the larger of 2,200 ms or 950 ms plus 275 ms per word plus 850 ms per number.

## §06 — Ghost Onions, colour science and render gates

Each Vessel's visual grammar splits into eight separable, versioned layers called Ghost Onions, modelled on the keepsake cabinet in Hades II. The layers are pacing, motion, layout, transitions, data grammar, typography, colour and emotional restraint. Each can be bumped or rolled back alone, so one weak layer no longer forces a full rewrite, which the old channel did from V24 to V31. Rules inside the layers: edge margins of at least 6 percent, with a reserved empty corner for the duration label; a carry score of at least 0.75 between cuts inside a movement, with hard 0.4-second resets only at major turns; at most three number formats per Vessel; at most two subtitle font sizes and three lines; and colour set in perceptual OKLCH space, with at least 8.5 to 1 contrast at 160 pixels. Motion forbids the default slow zoom on a still image. Counters use an ease-out curve, not linear growth. Before any act is marked complete, three frame checks run in under 0.8 seconds. A mute-progression gate rejects a 30-second window whose only motion is a uniform zoom. A structural-variety gate requires consecutive 60-second windows to change composition class. An edge-clip gate rejects any glyph inside the outer 5 percent. A failed beat reroutes to a live rig rather than shipping.

## §07 — Diviner ideation, Crossroads loops and Echo Decay

The Diviner holds twelve title equations, labelled T1 to T3 and EQ_01 to EQ_09. Each is a slot template with a hard constraint, a paired thumbnail collision and a benchmark. Examples: T1, an unbroken real-time journey with no time skips; T2, a two-number era comparison with exactly two sourced figures; EQ_02, a rival-to-a-titan benchmark. An equation earns crowned status after five uploads at 1.5 times median and drops to cooldown after two consecutive uploads below 0.7 times. The Crossroads engine runs four loops. Shadow Sieve compares a donor's winners (2.5 times or more) with its flops (below 0.5 times, older than 21 days) and keeps only words that separate them. Graft to Bloom has two phases. Up to 12 uploads, 85 percent of cards come from proven outliers at 3.0 times or more in the channel's neighbourhood. From upload 13, comment mining reads top comments on the channel's winners and rivals for viewer demands. Echo Decay fires at 2.0 times median and queues three follow-ups: about 90 percent match, then 70 percent with a niche-up move and a new thumbnail silhouette, then 50 percent keeping only the viewer's curiosity. A resting period follows. The spec gives that cooldown as 10 uploads here and 15 in section 09. A five-gate filter rejects weak ideas in under one second: a universal anchor, a Rising River check that the new audience is at least as large, no repeated title and thumbnail words, verifiable visual proof, and no collision with the Resting Shelf.

## §08 — Transcript forensics and the SpineDna absorber

The section decodes eight breakout channels active in late 2026, each with 280,000 to 3.1 million views, to show that a structural spine and a specific voice matter more than budget. Each maps to a preset spine. The examples range from a stoic mirror opening with a three-way close, to a second-person RPG walkthrough, to an engineering-bet structure, to a faction-chat chronicle that costs about $0.02 per film. The table uses SB_01 to SB_08, but the heading says SB_01 to SB_07, so the count is unresolved. The spec highlights SB_07, the world-log dispatch. Its cards run 15 to 22 seconds and have three lines: a physical clock or telemetry line, an institutional consequence with real friction such as rationing, and one ordinary human echo. A Scenario Part-2 steer lets an operator paste a video and type a short steer about a later date or split. The system pulls a world bible (ship names, physics limits, delays, tone) from the transcript, advances the timeline with the high-thinking outline pass, and deals three titles and three thumbnails for the new film. The SpineDna absorber takes one to three YouTube URLs and, in under four seconds, extracts a reusable schema. It holds a spine ID, a words-per-minute curve over four quartiles, a cold-open limit of 22 seconds, a station sequence of typed beats, a minimum causal-connector ratio of 0.72, an optional offering slot at a retention peak, and donor weights for fusing sources.

## §09 — Spirit Tongue voice and copy compiler

Voice has two layers that must not mix. Universal rules remove AI tics everywhere: negation-first reversals, meta-talk, population straw-men, fake statistics, brochure vocabulary and mechanical cadence. Persona rules switch per channel, because a ban that suits one channel would break a dialogue or world-dispatch channel. Stuffing thousands of ban words into the writer prompt caused fixation and repeated props across scripts, so the writer prompt stays short and the rules run in code. Layer zero is a Rust linter that checks each sentence in under 2 milliseconds against 42 tics, grouped into five categories. A hit flags one sentence; a healer rewrites all flagged sentences in one batched call under 1.6 seconds, and the rewrites are rechecked. Layer one holds six voice archetypes, VM_01 to VM_06: elder-brother counsel, dated world dispatch with no narrator "I", polyphonic faction stage, deadpan realm guide, instrument atlas tied to visual load, and value copy for offer pages. Layer two extracts a locked voice profile from three or more transcripts. Layer three is the Resting Shelf. After each script, named sources, physical props, clock hours and opening skeletons are stored for 15 uploads, and a 90-token exclusion list enters the outline pass. Caps: no more than 12 named sources per 60 minutes, and no signature vocative more than twice per script. Three delivery modes (voiced, diegetic radio, silent cinema) sit on top.

## §10 — Sigil packaging studio, Mirror Bend and the gate chain

Titles and thumbnails are solved together by Sigil, because image models handle camera and surface language, not essay prompts. Sigil uses six extensible dialects, D1 to D6, each with fixed slots for camera, asset and physics, and with text drawn in Rust so no model garbles words. D1 is photoreal observer reportage built on verified archival photos. D2 is a broadcast evidence desk with at most one arrow. D3 is a 3D architectural cutaway with zero to two words. D4 is a direct frame from the sim render, with zero generation cost. D5 is a staged story tableau. D6 is brand etching or a four-colour TV atlas. Mirror Bend is the one-click outlier transmuter. Pass one measures the reference's geometry (subject box, horizon tilt, void share, colour opposition, text count), not its pixels. Pass two renders three variants for about $0.057 in total: a direct homage, a mirrored layout with tighter focus, and a high-void, high-contrast cut. Feed Sight previews each candidate in four views: desktop grid, mobile feed, a 168 by 94 squint test, and a 65-inch TV grid with the duration label overlaid. Six gates run in under 400 ms: safe edges, luminance contrast, a 160 by 90 Otsu test requiring one to three foreground masses, a sibling-fatigue check against the previous upload, no title and thumbnail word duplication, and a face and logo integrity check.
