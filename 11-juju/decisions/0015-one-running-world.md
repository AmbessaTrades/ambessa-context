## What the lead got wrong

Swatches, font pairs, quote layouts and 24 stills are page design. Arranging better pages makes a better slideshow. The
blind test on stills would have rewarded exactly that. The owner's standing bar was already written: "never a slideshow
of stills" (USER.md).

## What was seen (lead looked at whole films, 24 Nov)

- `cEkozf-zCkY` (2.8M views, 66.7 times its channel's median): 14 of 16 frames over 16 minutes are one cockpit, one
  star ahead, dials running. `4R7VcBGVLH4`: the same from one craft. Jacopist (1.9M): one solar map, a date running.
- They move LESS than ours by pixel: median change 0.08 (Nearest Star), 0.18 (Jacopist) against 0.84 to 4.09 (our
  stage-y). So more motion is not the answer. Being inside one thing that is really happening is.
- Of the 22 highest multiples in outliers.json, at least 11 are one variable that keeps changing ("kept getting", "never
  stopped", "couldn't stop", "every step", "real time"). Caveat: that gallery was hand picked, so this shows the kind
  can win, not how often it does. No flops of the kind were counted.

## The film unit

**One viewpoint. One variable that keeps changing, by real physics, from a source. Consequences arrive in order.** No
pages, no cuts to cards, no arranged quotations. Type is instruments only: what the variable is now, and the clock.

1. Strong builders make the world once: a real renderer on the Mac's GPU (wgpu, `crates/juju-world`), light, air, lens.
2. The cheap model writes no pictures. It fills a dial table: value, what happens there, the number, the source.
3. Code computes the physics between the rows, moves the world, and refuses a row that contradicts it.
4. Sound is half of it: a bed driven by the same variable. The first film has no narration (silent channel, $0 voice).
5. Judged as clips beside published clips of the kind, on one question first: would you stay, and when do you leave.
6. A second film in the same world with a different variable, filled only by the cheap model, is the proof of leverage.

## Limit, said plainly

The judges read films as dense frame strips and measurements; they cannot feel motion or sound as a person does. The
owner's eye on a 20 second clip is the real test, so he sees one as soon as the judges stop finding faults of craft.
