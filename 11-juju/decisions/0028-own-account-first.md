# ADR 0028: a people's own account first, and how sure we are is shown

Working document, changes with conversation. Said by Ambi 26 Nov 2026, about 01:40, after the South America, Asia and world time maps.

1. What makes these channels stand out is how Juju finds, understands, joins and sources information "that aren't always talked about", and that Ambi can direct Juju to a kind of information.
2. Research and maps "should always bias towards" the information "of the indigenous people there", Africa and Latin America included; "real books and indigenous books". Today's maps "have like a Eurocentric lens".
3. Where living memory has only the outside version, bias "towards the oldest possible known record", in brackets if needed: "speculative but sourced or something like that".
4. A film says when its map is an estimate ("a map estimation warning") and carries "a scholarly odds estimate somewhere", small. A fictional or simulated film needs no such notes.
5. Examples: South America must not open at 1200 AD; the Indus and Kemet are far older than the set says.

Lead's reading, *open questions* put to Ambi on 26 Nov: what the odds are made of (a model may not invent a percent); what an outside source may still do; how an old account is drawn beside a dug and dated one. Facts the lead gave with them, not yet checked against a page: the Olmec are of Mexico; the oldest towns of the Andes are Caral's, about 3000 BC; the Turin papyrus counts tens of thousands of years before the first king.

Consequence: the Cliopatria set is one shelf among several, never the frame of a film by itself.

## Answers, 26 Nov about 02:00 (Ambi)

6. Sureness is not shown as counts ("3 of 4 sources agree" is "kind of corny"). Be confident in the sources; when it is needed, one short, clear, specific note ("this is disputed", or what exactly is uncertain), worded so it is plain why the note is there. Not a note on everything.
7. Land from a people's own older record may be drawn apart from dug and dated land (hatched, a bracket) "only if you do it in a way that doesn't take away from the power of the moment". Think it through to the second, third and fourth order for what the viewer feels (Kallaway).
8. Carbon dating and other dated finds are welcome and "extremely important". Juju Motion may bring up a real picture of the thing (a find, a scroll, a charter), linked to its place: the camera pans down, it rises, it goes back, the map goes on.
9. Lore and tradition are wanted too ("we can make some money" with them): a tradition's own count (Shiva 40,000 years ago, the age of the Olmec heads, the Indus) is told as that tradition's account. Simulation and lore films carry no evidence notes.

## Added 26 Nov about 10:40 (Ambi, on the narration line "The mansa kept every gold nugget; miners kept only dust")

10. That is not how the people of the region see it: Mali was complex and wealthy, and its miners were not poor. Juju's writing, in every niche, carries a bias against the conventional narrative and always tries to source the truth behind it: Africa painted as barbarous by scholars; in India, which dynasties are allowed to have been globally important (the Chola against northern lines) and who ruled long ago; the same in China, the Americas and Europe.
11. This needs layers: the storyboard, the script, the copy and the visuals go together. "That's what we mean when we say the Kallaway": fourth-layer human thinking (in Juju, the nyansapo ladder).
Lead's note, exploring: "against the conventional narrative" is held to the same sources as everything else: a counter-claim stands on the people's own record, dated finds, or a named scholar's case, told with its kind; it never replaces one unsourced story with another.
