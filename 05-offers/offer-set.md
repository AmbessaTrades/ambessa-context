## Why 1 and 2 are the same document read in opposite directions

- Read one way, a company asks: *will this government pay me, and can it even sign?*
- Read the other way, a government asks: *is this bidder real, and has it ever delivered?*
- Each check feeds the other. Company questions build a payment record; government checks build
  a delivery record. Held together they become the only two-sided record of who pays and who
  delivers in a market — which is the moat, not the software.

## The Authority File, expanded

Sells to procurement boards, permanent secretaries, auditors-general, anti-corruption
commissions and state-owned banks. The budget already exists, because donor-funded
procurement requires bidder due diligence before money is released.

Blind spots to design around:

1. **Two doors, one kitchen.** You cannot be seen selling to companies about a ministry and to
   that ministry about companies. Two brands, or one hard rule that you never name which side
   asked.
2. **Liability wording.** Never "this bidder is clean". Always "no record of X was found, and
   this is what could not be verified".
3. **The registry may not be public.** Then the honest product is a statement of what cannot be
   checked, sold as that.
4. **Twenty rulebooks.** Each country and each donor demands different checks. Encoding them is
   the real work and the real moat.
5. **Your own channel criticises ministers.** Sell to agencies and audit bodies, and keep every
   on-camera claim to documented facts.

## Deliberately dead

- Power maps that assert who controls whom — unverifiable, so indefensible.
- Landed-cost calculators — a lawyer or freight forwarder already owns that seat.
- Procurement opportunity lists — a list that commoditises.
- Voice-to-paper subscriptions at $30 a month — a tool, not a high-ticket product.
- A deal room as the first ask — it asks the buyer to change how he works on day one.
