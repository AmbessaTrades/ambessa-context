# What small competitors do and charge

_Observations only. What startups sell into these markets, at what price, and how they sell it. Nothing here is a recommendation._

204 verified findings in `pains.db`. This sheet is only what these businesses actually do and
charge. **Observations only.** No mechanic here is adopted, recommended or decided — those
calls are Ambi's, and nothing in this document is a proposal.

## 1. The lane check: who already works Africa–Caribbean?

This was your real question, and the answer is nearly nobody.

| Player | What they do | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **WapiPay** | The only startup found with a licensed live Africa→Caribbean corridor; uses **JN Money** as the Jamaican payout leg | Live, licensed by Bank of Jamaica |
| **Knova + Origins Logistics ACC** | Jamaica–Ghana trade corridor; customs declarations and compliance certificates | Building, no funding disclosed |
| **Zeepay (Barbados)** | Attempted a Caribbean beachhead | **Suspended by the central bank, then wound up** — the warning |
| **Lumepay** | Rail-agnostic API mixing bank rails and **stablecoins**; $6m pre-seed + $10m float | Live, six new markets |
| **Maplerad** | Lets a business stand up a licensed payments product in minutes; 8 countries | $6m seed |
| **Cloud9** | Trade documents, compliance and trade finance in one interface | $1m pre-seed |
| **Swiftway** (Accra) | Customs clearance, haulage and broker paperwork for SME importers | Bootstrapped |
| **PAS Cargo** (Guyana) | Caribbean-side mirror: consolidation, brokerage, warehousing | IDB Lab financing |

**Read:** the corridor is *live but unserved*. Two players at the edges, one corpse in the
middle. Nobody is doing the deal layer — documents, verification, and the four answers.

## 2. Pricing actually in use here (what they charge, and the shape of it)

- **Flat per seat, every module included.** Cirquu: **₦9,000 (~$7) per user per month** for a
  17-module platform, Growth ₦15,000, no setup fee. Flatness is the headline, not a footnote.
- **Charge the seat, not the AI.** Knowlix **$24.90/seat/mo** (discounted from $35.60), 7-day
  trial no card. Bujeti ₦17,000 / ₦22,500 / ₦35,000 per user per month, billed annually.
- **Credits for the heavy user, seats for the team.** Decide: **$7 for 500 credits**, Team
  **$35 for 4 seats** and 7,500 pooled credits.
- **Three rungs with the top one being "contact sales".** Lelapa: **$9.99 → $49.99 → call us.**
- **Local currency and mobile money, not USD cards.** Meditect (Côte d'Ivoire): **50,000 XOF
  (~$88)/year for 2 users, 200,000 XOF (~$350) unlimited**, paid by Mobile Money, with an
  install fee partly donor-funded.
- **Charge the transaction, not the subscription** where the money moves. VunaPay takes a fee
  per payout (140 cooperatives, 110,000 farmers). CoAmana: **$250/yr Direct, $7,500/yr
  Enterprise** for verified informal-market price data — the closest thing here to selling
  verified information.
- **Free tier is a customer-acquisition loan, and the revenue line sits underneath it.**
  Oze's Bronze is free forever and monetises elsewhere; Bumpa ran free to **10,000 paying
  businesses** on a public price list.
- **The entry rung is a card-backed 7–14 day trial**, not a free plan.

## 3. How they actually get customers (the transferable part)

1. **The founder DMs the decision-maker above the champion.** Bujeti closed its first
   enterprise account by messaging the group CEO directly after the finance team stalled it,
   then doing a live, in-person demo. Not a funnel — a person.
2. **Hand the buyer one public artefact so they can justify it internally.** Cirquu sells the
   founder's story (Forbes Africa feature, TechCabal profile) before the product. Decide got
   **1,000 users in 24 days with no marketing budget** — founder posting on X plus a public
   benchmark. Lelapa feeds its self-serve rungs with near-daily founder posting.
3. **Stagecraft is pipeline.** Askya pulled **1,250–1,300 applications** from one open call
   amplified by press and a Lagos Moonshot event. 11 were picked.
4. **Self-serve crosses borders without a sales team.** Knowlix entered **29 African countries**
   with a free trial, a $24.90 price and a syndicated content play — no local staff.
5. **One government partnership beats a hundred cold emails.** Awarri became Nigeria's national
   LLM builder because a minister announced it; the state supplied the data and the distribution.
6. **WhatsApp is the checkout, not a channel.** Bill Express (Jamaica): message one number,
   **J$55 per bill**, receipt in the chat, no app download. Zuri Health: **KES 250 per chat
   consultation** through a WhatsApp bot named Vera. In the Caribbean informal market the whole
   sale is a WhatsApp Status → DM → voice note → bank-transfer screenshot.

## 4. Faceless brands: where they win, and where they don't

They are real and they are charging:

| Brand | Offer | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Onzira Labs (Jamaica, Indian-run) | AI automation | Pilot **$2,500–6,000**; programme **$8,000–25,000**; retainer |
| ThinkAI Africa (Nairobi) | AI services, logo strip of generic-sounding clients | **$990 / $2,490 / custom** per month |
| Prescript Digital (Lagos) | Five services incl. AI agents | **$5,000 setup + $1,500/mo** |
| TwinIsle Digital (Trinidad) | WhatsApp assistants, automation | **TT$3,000/mo**, no founder, no address |
| Kwanex | Automation, four markets | "Built by an African founder" — **founder's name withheld** |
| AI Now Now (Lagos) | Voice AI + content | **79,999 naira (~$53)/mo** |

The mechanics they win with: **published USD prices with a local-currency conversion, a free
audit as the funnel, and a fixed-scope paid pilot** instead of a proposal.

But the honest finding: **not one of them shows a named client.** Their logos read as
placeholders. And the strongest Caribbean players do the opposite — **ChatFlow**, a five-person
Jamaican AI support firm, has **four government pilots** and won a J$4M patent grant from the
Development Bank of Jamaica; **Avance** states on its own homepage that it publishes no case
studies or ROI figures, and leads with founder honesty.

**What the evidence shows:** faceless price sheets hold the commodity end, and the named
founder holds the trust-heavy end where government and bank buyers are deciding. Nobody in
this sample is doing both at once.

## 5. The shape of it, as observed

Recorded, not prescribed — this is what the sample shows, in their words and their prices.

1. Productised offers are priced **flat and per seat**; deal-shaped work is priced per engagement.
2. Prices are shown in **local currency and mobile money**, not only USD cards.
3. The entry point is a **paid, fixed-scope pilot** (Onzira $2,500–6,000) or a **card-backed trial**, not a proposal document.
4. A **free audit or readiness assessment** is the most common front door (Kwanex, TwinIsle).
5. New business comes from the **founder contacting the principal directly**, followed by a live demo.
6. The buyer is handed **one forwardable artefact** — benchmark, press feature, or named dossier.
7. **One open call or public event per quarter** produces the pipeline that press then amplifies (Askya).
8. **WhatsApp is the front door** for Caribbean and West African mid-market buyers, not email.
9. Anyone moving money across a Caribbean border sits under a **licence** — Zeepay's Barbados arm lost it.
10. A **single government partnership** produced national distribution for Awarri; nobody in the sample reached that by posting alone.

---

## 6. Patterns that failed, in this sample

- **Per-module pricing** produces long feature grids and unclear entry prices.
- **Free-forever plans** only survive where a lending or referral revenue line sits underneath.
- **Generic logo strips** read as placeholders; none of the faceless brands shows a verifiable named client.
- **Building a payment rail from scratch** is where the capital went — Lumepay, Maplerad, enza, WapiPay and PAPSS already occupy it.
