# The market answers

_Who is already in these markets and what they are doing: East Africa's interest, China's method, India's opening, settlement reality, where contracts are tendered._

Six questions, answered from documents. 140 findings collected, **130 machine-verified**
(source re-opened, quote still there). Files: `~/AUH/AMBESSA/intel/`.

---

## The short answer to each

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is East Africa interested in the Caribbean? | **Politically yes. Commercially, no.** Trade is a rounding error and every agreement is unimplemented. |
| What are the Chinese building in Africa? | **Financed surveillance, telecoms and consumer finance.** Vendor chosen before the tender opens. |
| What are the Indians doing? | **Sovereignty IT and payments, sold explicitly as the non-Chinese option.** And it is winning. |
| Are Chinese/Indian devs shipping daily? | **Partly true — but they win at the device preload and ad spend, not by living in Nairobi.** |
| Are stablecoins/tokenised rails the answer? | **Real, growing, still tiny.** Volumes are millions, not billions. Use them, don't build them. |
| Where are the government contracts? | **Published and open, and almost all require a local entity to bid.** |

## 1. East Africa: political will, no commercial machinery

- The CARICOM chair opened the first Africa-CARICOM summit by saying there had **never been
  trade between the two regions at all**.
- Of the forum's US$300m of announced deals, **not one had an East African counterparty** —
  they were Caribbean borrowers taking Afreximbank lending.
- A Kenya–Barbados **Air Services Agreement and joint trade committee were signed in 2021**.
  No measurable trade, no route, seven years after Kenya's president promised direct flights.
- The **EAC's own trade data** shows its export gravity is Asia (56%) and intra-Africa (28%).
  The Caribbean does not register.
- Kagame told Jamaica's parliament in 2022 he wanted closer trade and investment. Nothing
  material followed.
- Tanzania sent its Prime Minister to the Caribbean forum and asked for better investment
  terms rather than announcing a deal.
- Uganda's diaspora push is aimed at **inbound American capital and tourism**, not two-way trade.
- Kenya is opening a resident High Commission in Kingston in 2026 — a political bet that a
  near-zero relationship can be turned into CARICOM access.

**Read:** the permission slip is signed and nobody has used it. That is a better position than
entering a crowded lane — but it also means there is no demand to inherit. We would be creating
the first deal, not taking share.

## 2. China: they buy the tender before it opens

- **Over $2bn** spent on Chinese facial-recognition and plate-tracking systems across **11
  countries**; Nigeria alone **over $470m**. Typically bundled into soft loans that **name the
  hardware and the command centre** — so the vendor is decided before the procurement starts.
- **ZTE built Ethiopia's entire national telecom network** under a **$1.5bn** China Development
  Bank supplier credit.
- **Huawei** holds Kenya's Safe City (with Safaricom), **Uganda Police's $126m** CCTV network,
  public-cloud regions in Johannesburg and Cape Town, and Zambia's first "smart village" —
  where it is now pushing **DeepSeek** models into government services.
- **CloudWalk** signed with Zimbabwe to build a national facial-recognition database.
- **Transsion** ships about **half of Africa's smartphones** and preinstalls apps on 20m
  handsets: PalmPay (a $40m Transsion-led round) and OPay (40k agents, $5m a day).

**Read:** we cannot win a price fight or a state-credit fight, and we should never be a hardware
vendor. Their weakness is that they are the lender — which means they are also the party a
ministry cannot complain about.

## 3. India: selling "not Chinese" and winning

- **Ghana bought a >$200m Indian 5G stack** (Radisys/Jio with Tech Mahindra) explicitly because
  it did not want to hand the network to Chinese vendors.
- **NPCI licensed its UPI design to the Bank of Namibia** for instant payments and grant
  disbursement.
- **TCS is building a sovereign cloud in Kenya** with Sybyl and iXAfrica; **Infosys Finacle** is
  replacing Investec's core banking across four jurisdictions; **Zoho** localised for Kenyan
  VAT and the TIMS invoicing system.
- Guyana's four-lane road came on an **Indian Exim line that required Indian contractors** —
  proof that tied finance buys the work, the same lever China uses.
- Their soft spot: holding African assets. Kenya pushed **Tata Chemicals out of Lake Magadi**
  after a century-long concession; **Cipla sold control** of Uganda's QCIL.

**Read:** the market already rewards a credible non-Chinese, sovereignty-respecting partner.
India occupies that space for big IT. **Nobody occupies it for the mid-market deal.**

## 4. "They're shipping every day" — partly true, and not the threat it feels like

- **Four of Ghana's top five Android shopping apps at end-2025 were Chinese-owned**; Temu went
  from 5,600 to over **208,000 downloads in one quarter** — bought with ads and discounting.
- In **Jamaica**, Chinese marketplaces are the main source of inbound parcels, with no local
  storefront and no resident team.
- In **Nairobi**, small Chinese AI firms modify open-weight Chinese models for local businesses
  and charge below what they would earn at home.
- Distribution is won at the **preload** (Transsion handsets) and at the **ad auction**.
- And it fails too: **ByteDance's LetsChat was shut three years in** after failing to dislodge WhatsApp.

**Read:** their advantage is capital and channel, not presence or trust. We cannot outspend Temu.
We do not have to — a Guyanese ministry does not buy a minister's counterparty from a Temu listing.

## 5. Settlement: the rails are real and the volumes are small

- **Onafriq is putting USDC into live settlement** because **more than 80% of intra-African
  payments still route through banks outside the continent**, a **$5bn-a-year** fee pool.
- **PAPSS** is live in **30+ countries** with value growth around **120% year on year** — but
  **Nigeria settled only N5.6bn (~$3.6m) in H1 2025, down 53%**, across 3,246 transactions.
- The Caribbean is building a **PAPSS-modelled CAPSS** with African technical support, and the
  **ECCB shelved DCash 2.0** to fund it. Pre-pilot, no volumes.
- Regulation is uneven: Ghana has a licensing statute that covers stablecoins; **Kenya had
  licensed no virtual asset provider at all** months after its law commenced; South Africa has
  **310 licensed CASPs** but still does not treat crypto as currency; Nigerian banks may hold
  VASP accounts but cannot deal on their own book.
- Africa–Caribbean trade has **never exceeded 6%** of bilateral exports; the stated target is
  **+$2.1bn in five years**.

**Read:** settlement is being solved slowly by others with far more capital. We should sit on
top of it — quote the $5bn and the 80% in content, and use whatever rail settles — never build one.

## 6. Where the contracts are — and the door rule

Live examples found and verified this week:

- **Kenya ICT Authority** — three-lot national Integrated Government Resource Platform
  (interoperability, identity federation, digital signing), World Bank 7289-KE / 7290-KE.
- **Rwanda** — Shared Government Data Hub under the World Bank Digital Acceleration Project;
  a national SOC upgrade (Credit 7000-RW); Digital Situation Room consultancy (KfW 514011).
- **Nigeria** — **$2bn, 90,000 km fibre** pre-qualification, must be delivered through a
  **Nigerian special purpose vehicle**; $800m already secured.
- **Guyana** — national e-procurement build; every bid must carry local **NIS and GRA**
  certificates, so a foreign supplier cannot bid without a Guyanese presence.
- **Jamaica** — health ministry DHIS2/TSIS2 interoperability, submitted only through GOJEP
  with a Tax Compliance Certificate.
- **South Africa** — every supplier, including foreign vendors, must register on the National
  Treasury **Central Supplier Database**, not with SARS.
- **AfDB** — buying a study to redesign the **SACU customs union** tariff and revenue pool.
- **Trinidad (TSTT)** — 36-month managed service, tendered on ProcureTT.

Portal reality, tested: Kenya's `tenders.go.ke` document store works while its homepage is a
JS shell; Nigeria's BPP works, NOCOPO is dead; Ghana's PPA/GHANEPS work; Rwanda's RISA works
while umucyo is a shell; Guyana's NPTAB and Barbados' portal are blocked; South Africa's
eTenders hides tender rows behind JS.

**Read:** every door has a local-entity lock. That is not an obstacle to our model — it is the
argument for it. We partner and hold the room; we do not bid.

## 7. The gap nobody is standing in

- **China** is a financed hardware and surveillance vendor. Its incumbency is a loan.
- **India** is a sovereignty IT outsourcer for banks and governments.
- **Chinese and Indian consumer apps** are buying distribution with capital.

None of them is the **trusted middle for one mid-market cross-border deal**: verifying both
sides, answering who confirms the credit, who funds the gap, who carries it — and holding the
file. That layer is not a capital game, so China cannot outspend it, and it is not a systems
tender, so India is not in it.

And the political permission is already granted on paper: summits, air services agreements,
high commissions, a free trade arrangement "endorsed for establishment" — with **no commercial
machinery underneath any of it**.

## 8. What this means for the 22nd

1. Lead with the absence, not the opportunity: *the two regions have never traded; every
   agreement is unimplemented; nobody operates the middle.*
2. Never position as an AI company. Position as the desk that makes one deal survivable.
3. Use the Chinese and Indian record as content ammunition — $2bn of surveillance on soft
   loans, a $200m 5G deal won by being not-Chinese, Temu buying Ghana with ads. That is the
   material for the daily videos, and it is all documented.
4. Quote the money: $5bn a year in intra-African payment fees, 80% of payments routed offshore,
   $74bn trade finance gap, 6% ceiling on Africa–Caribbean trade.
5. The 22nd is the coding sprint; this is the evidence it will be pointed at.
