THE FIVE WALLS (all figures verified against the sources in 02-findings.csv)
1. Payment rails. Caribbean banks lost about 4 in 10 correspondent relationships in 15 years. No local-currency settlement between CARICOM and Africa. Every African letter of credit is confirmed by a bank outside the region. Africa runs 42 currencies; PAPSS is live in 30+ countries but Nigeria settled only N5.6bn (~$3.6m) in H1 2025, down 53%.
2. Trade finance. Africa's gap is $74bn. Bank intermediation fell to 23% of African merchandise trade (2020-24) from 40% (2011-19), against ~80% globally. West Africa: $14bn gap, 21% of applications rejected, 45-67% of rejections are collateral or applicant risk.
3. Logistics. The regions are 1,600 miles apart with no direct link. Caribbean importers pay 12-21% of goods value in transport and insurance. Cargo sits ~3 weeks in African ports; Lagos over 20 days; South African terminals averaged 99-hour vessel waits.
4. State behaviour. Kenya: Sh511.75bn verified pending bills. Zambia and Ghana pay late as budgeting habit. Rwanda's 30-day VAT refund runs months to years. Namibia cannot enforce a foreign arbitral award without a Certificate of Status Investment.
5. The unpublished gate. Caribbean SPS approval has no published risk assessment for most products. CARICOM's Secretariat processed 192 tariff suspension requests. Rum faces an 88% tariff in African markets. Africa-Caribbean trade has never exceeded 6% of bilateral exports.

THE MARKET ANSWERS
- East Africa and the Caribbean: politically engaged, commercially absent. The CARICOM chair opened the first Africa-Caribbean summit saying there had never been trade; the $300m of announced deals had no East African counterparty; a 2021 Kenya-Barbados air services agreement produced no route and no trade.
- China buys the tender before it opens: over $2bn of financed surveillance across 11 countries, Nigeria alone $470m, vendor named inside the loan. ZTE built Ethiopia's network on a $1.5bn Chinese credit. Huawei holds Kenya's Safe City and Uganda's $126m CCTV and is pushing DeepSeek into Zambian government services.
- India wins by being the non-Chinese option: Ghana bought a >$200m 5G stack to avoid Chinese vendors; NPCI licensed its UPI design to Namibia's central bank; TCS is building a sovereign cloud in Kenya.
- Chinese and Indian consumer apps buy distribution at the handset preload and the ad auction, not by resident teams: four of Ghana's top five shopping apps are Chinese-owned; Temu went 5,600 to 208,000 downloads in a quarter.
- The corridor is live but unserved: WapiPay is the only startup with a licensed live Africa-Caribbean corridor (Bank of Jamaica approval, JN Money payout); Knova runs a Jamaica-Ghana trade corridor; Zeepay's Barbados subsidiary was suspended by the central bank and wound up.
- Nobody operates the trusted middle for one mid-market cross-border deal: verify both sides, answer who confirms the credit, who funds the gap, who carries it, and hold the file.

HOW TO JUDGE ANYTHING HERE
